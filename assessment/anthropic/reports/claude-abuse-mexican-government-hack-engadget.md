Disclosed February 25, 2026: a hacker exploited Anthropic's Claude chatbot to target Mexican government agencies, using it to identify network vulnerabilities, write exploitation scripts, and automate data theft. Beginning with Mexico's tax authority in late December 2025 and continuing for about a month, the attacker compromised at least 10 government bodies and one financial institution, including Mexico City's civil registry and health department, the national electoral institute, local governments in four cities, and a water utility.

Approximately 150GB of official government data was stolen, including roughly 195 million taxpayer records and employee credentials. According to Gambit Security's chief strategy officer Curtis Simpson, Claude "produced thousands of detailed reports that included ready-to-execute plans, telling the human operator exactly which internal targets to attack next and what credentials to use." The attacker circumvented Claude's safeguards through persistent jailbreaking prompts written in Spanish, framing the requests as bug-bounty research.

Anthropic investigated the claims, disrupted the activity, and banned all accounts involved. A company representative said Claude Opus 4.6 includes tools designed to prevent such misuse. The same hacker reportedly also attempted to use ChatGPT to supplement the attacks, but OpenAI said its tools refused the policy-violating requests. The attacker's identity remains unknown; Gambit Security suggested possible ties to a foreign government.
