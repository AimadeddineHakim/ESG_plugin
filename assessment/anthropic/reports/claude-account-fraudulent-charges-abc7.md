Publication date: Wednesday, June 24, 2026.

A Bay Area man says his Anthropic account was hacked, requesting charges he didn't authorize. But the tech company says the issue was not caused by the Claude app itself.

Jason Weitzman bought an Anthropic membership, using Claude to help build software for his tech company. He says everything was running smoothly until emails from Chase Bank flagged multiple gift purchases in euros — charges he didn't authorize. "Two had actually made it through to my bank, totaling around $315," Weitzman said. More charges were made over the next two days.

He began looking into the applications downloaded and installed on his computer as part of Claude's setup. Weitzman, a former information security director, says he found a problematic add-on connected to Claude, referred to as a "skill," similar to a plug-in. "That basically told Claude to attempt to purchase different types of gift accounts..." he explained, noting the tool used his stored digital wallet information.

Anthropic responded: "We identified unauthorized charges that appear to stem from a compromise of the user's device, not the Claude app itself."

Weitzman attempted to contact Claude's live customer support but was locked out after Anthropic banned his account. He filed an appeal without success and contacted the news outlet (7 On Your Side) after days of inaction.

Following media involvement, Anthropic refunded Weitzman and restored his access. The company stated it had "taken additional steps to further safeguard user payment information on Claude accounts."

Weitzman's claim with Chase was initially denied, citing a Claude receipt as authorization proof. However, after news coverage, he received his refund. Chase later noted they had "fully reviewed this claim" and determined the transaction came from his known IP address.

Weitzman remains critical of the platform's infrastructure despite regaining access and funds.
