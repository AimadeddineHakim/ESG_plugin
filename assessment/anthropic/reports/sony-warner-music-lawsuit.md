# Sony Music and Warner Sue Anthropic Over Copyright Violations

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell have filed a lawsuit against Anthropic and co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. The complaint, filed on Friday, accuses the AI company of conducting a "brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works."

## The Allegations

The publishers claim Anthropic engaged in "blatant theft" by utilizing thousands of protected compositions to train its Claude AI model. The lawsuit specifically charges the company with "flagrant piracy" through unauthorized torrenting to obtain millions of copies of books containing musical lyrics and sheet music. Other reporting (Fortune, Variety) notes the suit covers alleged theft of "tens of thousands" of songs, including works by The Beatles, Taylor Swift, and Michael Jackson, and seeks up to $150,000 per infringed work plus $25,000 per instance of removed copyright management information.

## Anthropic's Response

When reached for comment, an Anthropic spokesperson stated: "We disagree with the publishers' claims and we intend to defend ourselves robustly in court."

## Context of Ongoing Legal Battles

This represents another major intellectual property challenge for Anthropic. The same legal team previously handled cases brought by Concord Music Group and Universal Music Group in January. Additionally, Anthropic faced the high-profile Bartz v. Anthropic case involving authors who alleged copyright infringement. A judge determined that while using copyrighted material for training could be lawful, acquiring that content through piracy constitutes illegal activity. Anthropic ultimately settled that case for $1.5 billion.
