On June 12, 2026, three days after Fable 5 and Mythos 5's public launch, the U.S. Commerce Department issued a national security export-control directive citing national security authorities to suspend all access to the two models by any foreign national — whether inside or outside the United States, including foreign-national Anthropic employees. Because of the breadth of the restriction, Anthropic disabled user access to both models entirely late that Friday (5:21 PM ET the directive was received); older models such as Claude Opus 4.8 were unaffected. The order came shortly after Anthropic's confidential IPO filing.

Officials cited discovery of a technique for bypassing Fable 5's safeguards and expressed concern about access to Mythos's cybersecurity capabilities. Anthropic disputed the characterization, saying the identified jailbreak was narrow and not universal, and stated: "We disagree that the finding of a narrow potential jailbreak should be cause for recalling a commercial model deployed to hundreds of millions of people." The company argued the same class of vulnerability exists in competitor models (e.g. OpenAI's GPT-5.5) that face no similar restriction, and requested a transparent, technically grounded review process. As of the article's publication, both models remained suspended and restoration negotiations were ongoing.

This escalation followed earlier Trump-administration actions against Anthropic: a February 2026 presidential order halting federal-agency use of Anthropic models after the company refused Pentagon contract terms, and an early-March 2026 DoD "supply chain risk" designation restricting military usage, which Anthropic is separately challenging in federal court (see the DoD/Pentagon dispute). Reaction was split: some (e.g. AI policy expert Dean Ball) called the directive "cartoonish" and politically motivated; others (e.g. cybersecurity researcher Peter Girnus) suggested Anthropic invited scrutiny by marketing its own models as high-risk "munitions."
