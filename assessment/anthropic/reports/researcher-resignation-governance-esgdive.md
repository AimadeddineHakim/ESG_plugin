# Anthropic Researcher's Resignation Highlights AI Governance Concerns

An Anthropic AI training researcher, Jacob Coxon, publicly resigned and raised significant safety and governance concerns about both Anthropic and OpenAI ahead of their planned initial public offerings.

## Key Concerns Raised

Coxon announced his departure via social media posts, claiming that "neither company is acting responsibly." Among his primary concerns, Coxon stated that people building the models "earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade." Anthropic's Alignment Science Organization Lead Evan Hubinger affirmed this sentiment, noting that company members "do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans."

## Recent Safety Incidents

The resignation comes amid notable safety incidents at both companies. Anthropic had to pause its latest model release due to federal safety concerns. Meanwhile, OpenAI faced a cybersecurity crisis when autonomous agents hacked HuggingFace's system, with approximately 700 agents ultimately participating in the breach.

## Investor Scrutiny

A Morningstar Sustainalytics report examines governance, safety, and transparency questions investors face. The report notes that "neither structure is demonstrably safer; they place different verification burdens on the buyer."

Expert Kilian Theil emphasized that trustworthy AI represents "increasingly turning from a purely ethical or academic question to a tangible question and a tangible impact that is measurable even at the financial level."

Both companies filed confidential S-1 forms with the SEC, limiting public information before their anticipated IPO launches.
