# Anthropic's $1.5B Copyright Settlement Approved

A federal judge gave final approval on Monday to Anthropic's landmark $1.5 billion settlement resolving a class action copyright lawsuit filed by authors and book publishers.

Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California signed off on the settlement. The payout will distribute $3,000 per work across approximately 500,000 works, shared among rights holders.

## The Core Issue

The case centered on how Anthropic obtained books for training its AI model. Judge William Alsup (now retired) had previously ruled that Anthropic illegally downloaded and stored millions of copyrighted books from pirate sites like Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror, alongside legitimately purchased and scanned volumes.

Crucially, Alsup sided with Anthropic on a broader question: "training an AI model on copyrighted text counts as fair use." However, he found the acquisition method itself illegal, leading Anthropic to settle rather than face trial.

## Limited Precedent

While considered the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history, the resolution has narrow legal reach. Since Anthropic settled, the case never reached appeals court, meaning Alsup's ruling remains a single district court decision without binding precedent industrywide.

Other copyright lawsuits continue against Google, Meta, Midjourney, and OpenAI, with judges free to reach different conclusions on their own facts.
