# Kentucky Data Center Project Claims Environmental Compliance

TeraWulf, a Maryland-based developer, has submitted an energy plan to Kentucky regulators asserting that its Justified Data Campus in Hancock County will meet Governor Andy Beshear's environmental standards for data center projects.

## Project Details

The facility will be constructed on the site of a defunct aluminum smelter. TeraWulf maintains that "the project will have minimal environmental impact, using less water than the shuttered aluminum plant" while consuming comparable electricity levels to its predecessor operation.

The company estimates the data center will generate approximately 1,000 construction jobs and roughly 100 permanent positions upon completion. Anthropic, an artificial intelligence firm, has committed a $10 billion investment through a 20-year contract with TeraWulf.

## Environmental and Economic Claims

According to the filing, the facility will not increase electricity costs for residential customers. TeraWulf projects annual tax contributions of $7 million to the local school district, $14 million in state sales tax on electricity purchases, and property tax payments exceeding $22 million yearly.

The project utilizes an existing 482-megawatt power connection previously serving the aluminum operation. A power curtailment agreement allows utilities to reduce electricity supply during peak demand periods.

## Community Response

Residents expressed concerns about water consumption, air and noise pollution, and potential electricity rate increases during a Kentucky Public Service Commission hearing. A second public meeting is scheduled for the following month at a local elementary school.
