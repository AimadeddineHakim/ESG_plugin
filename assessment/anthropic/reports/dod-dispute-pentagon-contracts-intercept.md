Via a FOIA lawsuit, The Intercept obtained over 400 pages of documents on U.S. military contracts with Anthropic and its competitors, published September 8, 2026. In July 2025, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and xAI each signed contracts worth up to $200 million with the Department of Defense to develop AI prototypes intended to "improve military advantage, military utility, or enhance military decision making" across armed-forces applications, including reciprocal data-sharing provisions (the companies gave DoD/CDAO access to their models in exchange for sensitive operational briefings).

The relationship broke down in 2026 during negotiations over follow-up amendments that would have allowed deployment on classified military networks: Anthropic refused to sign without contractual prohibitions on domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons use, terms the Pentagon rejected. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth designated Anthropic a "supply chain risk" in March 2026, banning its services from military use; a federal judge overturned that ban in August 2026. Despite the reversal, as of this September 2026 reporting the DoD had rapidly finalized classified-network deployment agreements with Anthropic's competitors (OpenAI, Google, xAI), while Anthropic — which had rejected those terms — remained excluded from that classified integration.
