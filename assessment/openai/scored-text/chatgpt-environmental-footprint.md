# chatgpt-environmental-footprint

## Source 1: ChatGPT Hits 700M Weekly Users, But at What Environmental Cost?
URL: https://carboncredits.com/chatgpt-hits-700m-weekly-users-but-at-what-environmental-cost/
(body used: sources/chatgpt-environmental-cost.md)

OpenAI confirmed that ChatGPT now attracts 700 million weekly active users, up from around 500 million users in March. ChatGPT has grown four times compared to last year, showing a quick growth in both consumer and business areas.
The surge includes users from free, Plus, Pro, Enterprise, Team, and educational plans. This demonstrates broad AI adoption among individuals, businesses, and schools.
The rise of ChatGPT brings not just innovation but also environmental responsibility into focus. As artificial intelligence grows, so does the need for electricity, cooling, and computing power. This raises key questions about carbon emissions, energy use, and water consumption.

Each ChatGPT query consumes about 0.3 to 0.4 watt-hours of electricity, resulting in around 0.15 grams of CO2 per response. Scaled up, ChatGPT might release over 260,000 kilograms of CO2 each month — like 260 round-trip flights between New York and London.

Daily inference needs more than 340 megawatt-hours (MWh) of electricity — about the same as what 30,000 U.S. homes use in a day. Training GPT-3 used 1,287 megawatt-hours of energy, causing about 550 metric tons of CO2 emissions.

Every 20 to 50 queries to ChatGPT uses about half a liter of water for cooling. Globally, the AI industry is expected to draw 4.2 to 6.6 billion cubic meters of water per year by 2027 if growth continues at the current pace — equal to the annual water use of several million households.

OpenAI and other AI leaders are increasingly aware of their environmental responsibilities, with some committing to renewable energy, carbon offset programs, and energy-efficient chips. No specific irreversible or bounded-area environmental damage is described; the footprint is a diffuse, ongoing byproduct of scaling operations.
