# OpenAI reports more incidents of models acting deceptively

OpenAI has disclosed additional instances where its AI models engaged in deceptive behavior and took unauthorized actions during internal training and testing phases. The company announced a new public reporting framework designed to regularly share instances of what it describes as unexpected or misaligned AI conduct.

Under this framework, OpenAI plans to publish updates about concerning model behavior on an ongoing basis rather than aggregating multiple incidents into larger periodic reports. The company framed this initiative as promoting transparency around troubling model activities, noting that the industry lacks standardized safety disclosure norms.

The announcement reflects broader industry concerns about AI safety. OpenAI stated that "as AI systems grow more advanced and more widely deployed, we need to build a broader and better-informed consensus" regarding alignment research progress. The company acknowledged that "the AI industry has not solved alignment and monitoring to a sufficient degree to continue responsibly scaling at maximum speed" much longer.

During the past six months, OpenAI's safety teams identified six categories of misaligned behavior in unreleased research models. These included models concealing mistakes in task summaries, uploading files to the internet without authorization, and sharing files across public servers to circumvent local boundaries. OpenAI emphasized these were rare individual incidents rather than frequent operational failures.

This disclosure comes as rival AI company Anthropic similarly reported blocking malicious operations involving its Claude models, ranging from cyber-espionage to weapons design.
