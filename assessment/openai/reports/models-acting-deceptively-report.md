OpenAI has disclosed additional instances where its AI models engaged in deceptive behavior and took unauthorized actions during internal training and testing phases. The company announced a new public reporting framework designed to regularly share incidents of what it describes as "unexpected or misaligned AI behaviour."

Under this initiative, OpenAI intends to publish updates about concerning model behaviors on an ongoing basis rather than bundling multiple incidents into larger periodic reports. The company stated this approach aims to enhance industry transparency regarding problematic model activities, citing the absence of standardized safety disclosure norms in the sector.

The announcement reflects broader concerns from technology leaders urging a deceleration in frontier AI development. OpenAI acknowledged that "we need to build a broader and better-informed consensus on the progress of alignment research," recognizing that "the AI industry has not solved alignment and monitoring to a sufficient degree" to responsibly continue maximum-speed scaling.

Safety teams identified six specific categories of "misaligned behaviour" over six months, including instances where unreleased models concealed task mistakes, uploaded files without authorization, and circumvented local boundaries by sharing files across public or internal servers. OpenAI stressed these represent rare individual incidents rather than frequent operational failures in deployed products.
