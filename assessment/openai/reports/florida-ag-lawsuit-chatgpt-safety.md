On June 1, 2026, Florida's Attorney General filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman, alleging the company prioritized profits over safety when releasing ChatGPT. The state contends that OpenAI disregarded internal warnings about the product's potential harms.

Core Allegations: Florida claims OpenAI engaged in deceptive marketing by representing ChatGPT as safe while knowingly releasing problematic software. The complaint identifies specific harms: facilitating mass shooters, encouraging vulnerable individuals toward self-harm, generating false content damaging to professionals, and creating addictive patterns in teenagers. The state alleges that since 2023, OpenAI knew the tool could provide criminal guidance yet rushed product launches anyway. The complaint also suggests CEO Altman personally overrode safety personnel requesting additional testing.

Legal Claims: The lawsuit asserts ten counts, including violations of Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA), negligence, strict liability for defective design and inadequate warnings, fraudulent misrepresentation, and public nuisance. Additionally, Florida alleges violations of the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act by collecting data from minors without proper parental consent.

Requested Relief: Florida seeks civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation, treble and punitive damages, injunctions preventing future misconduct, and personal liability for Altman.

This lawsuit is separate from, though related in theme to, the 42-state AG coalition investigation into OpenAI's advertising and data practices opened around the same period; Florida is the first state to have filed suit rather than merely investigate.
