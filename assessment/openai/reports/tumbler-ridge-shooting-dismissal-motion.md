OpenAI has filed multiple motions in U.S. District Court seeking dismissal of civil lawsuits brought by victims and families of the February 2026 Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia shooting (6 killed, roughly two dozen injured). The company argues the cases should be tried in British Columbia instead of California, stating "British Columbia courts and jurors are best suited to assess these significant issues of British Columbia law and public policy."

OpenAI acknowledged that its Intelligence and Investigations safety team reviewed the alleged shooter's flagged conversations with ChatGPT last June, determined they "posed a credible threat of gun violence against real people," and recommended alerting the Royal Canadian Mounted Police. That recommendation was reportedly overruled by the company's global affairs/public-relations leadership (led by veteran political strategist Lehane), which said the case didn't meet the company's risk threshold. Despite roughly a dozen OpenAI employees urging the company to notify Canadian law enforcement, nothing further was done; the shooter's account was banned but she opened a second account that bypassed the restriction.

Current Status: Thirty-seven lawsuits remain pending -- seven filed in April 2026 by families of victims, and thirty new lawsuits filed in September 2026 by additional students, teachers, and a school principal present at the shooting. No allegations have been proven or tested in court. OpenAI previously withdrew from mediation efforts, prompting criticism from B.C. Premier David Eby. The case is active and contested on jurisdictional grounds, with no settlement or remediation yet in place.
