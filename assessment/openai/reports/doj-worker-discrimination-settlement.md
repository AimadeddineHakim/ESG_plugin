The Justice Department's Civil Rights Division announced a $3.2 million settlement with OpenAI OpCo LLC and its subsidiary Statsig Inc. The settlement addresses allegations that both companies violated the Immigration and Nationality Act by discriminating against U.S. workers in favor of those with temporary employment visas during the Permanent Labor Certification (PERM) process.

According to the investigation, OpenAI failed to advertise PERM positions on its external job website, required paper applications for those roles while accepting electronic submissions for other positions, and took additional steps to discourage U.S. applicants -- including advertising positions on late-night radio.

Assistant Attorney General Harmeet K. Dhillon stated: "It is illegal to discriminate against U.S. workers by preferring temporary visa [holders]."

The settlement requires OpenAI to pay $1.2 million in civil penalties and establish a $2 million back-pay fund for affected workers. The company must post positions publicly and accept electronic applications, train personnel on anti-discrimination requirements, and submit to departmental monitoring.

The announcement notes this is the thirteenth settlement under the Department's Protecting U.S. Workers Initiative, relaunched in 2025 to enforce the INA's anti-discrimination provisions. OpenAI said it disagrees with the Justice Department's conclusions but reached the agreement to resolve the matter and move forward.
