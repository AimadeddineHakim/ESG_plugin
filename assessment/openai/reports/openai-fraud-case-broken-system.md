Background: In 2015, Elon Musk contributed $38 million to OpenAI, a nonprofit pledging to develop artificial intelligence benefiting humanity. However, internal documents later revealed doubts about this commitment. Greg Brockman wrote in his private diary that "it was a lie" regarding the nonprofit promise.

Key Timeline: 2017 -- Brockman's diary entry questioned the nonprofit commitment. 2019 -- OpenAI established a for-profit subsidiary and secured $1 billion from Microsoft. 2025 -- that subsidiary reached a $500 billion valuation. Present -- the nonprofit Musk funded now controls less than one-third of the company.

Outcome: A federal judge allowed the fraud case to proceed to trial in April 2026. Musk alleged fraud, while Altman argued the changes reflected business necessity. (Note: the case went to trial in April-May 2026 and a jury found for OpenAI/Altman on statute-of-limitations grounds -- see companion source for the verdict.)

Underlying Tension: Former Chief Scientist Ilya Sutskever questioned Altman's motivations, asking whether "AGI [is] truly your primary motivation?" This highlighted organizational misalignment between stated mission and operational direction.

The author uses this case to argue that traditional nonprofit structures create inherent conflicts between maintaining mission integrity and achieving financial scale.
