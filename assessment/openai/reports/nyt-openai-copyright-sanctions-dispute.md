The New York Times, Daily News, and other U.S. media outlets are requesting that a federal judge impose penalties on OpenAI, intensifying a dispute regarding artificial intelligence and copyright protection that may significantly influence the future of the news industry.

These newspapers contend that OpenAI is concealing evidence relevant to a potentially significant copyright infringement case. The dispute centers on how OpenAI and Microsoft utilized millions of news articles to develop their AI systems. The core question involves whether AI chatbots constitute unfair competition by operating as information sources, drawing web traffic away from publishers without performing the journalistic work necessary to gather news.

In a court filing submitted July 9, 2026 in Manhattan, the plaintiffs argue that OpenAI "chose obstruction" rather than releasing datasets and ChatGPT conversation logs demonstrating how the AI system utilized copyrighted journalism. They request the judge penalize the company for "discovery misconduct" that could compromise evidence integrity, citing a recent deposition from an OpenAI representative that contradicts the company's prior statements.

Steven Lieberman, representing the Daily News and seven affiliated publications, asserts that OpenAI has been "making misrepresentations" for approximately two years concerning its capacity to identify copyrighted content in AI training information.

OpenAI responded through spokesperson Drew Pusateri, stating: "As the Times' case weakens and they've been forced to drop claims against us, they're persisting with efforts to invade the privacy of people who have nothing to do with this case."

The lawsuit, initiated by the Times in late 2023, also names Microsoft as a defendant. The sanctions motion is pending decision; no ruling has yet been issued.
