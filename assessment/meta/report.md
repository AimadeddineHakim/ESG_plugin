## 1. Meta AI Smart Glasses Privacy Lawsuit — Orange Flag (Score: 1, Severity: Severe)

**Category / Pillar:** Social — Customers : Privacy & Data Security

**Nature of Harm:** Serious — intimate, highly sensitive footage (nudity, sexual activity, financial details, identifiable faces) captured via smart glasses was viewed by low-paid overseas contractors without adequate disclosure.

**Scale of Impact:** Extensive — over 7 million units sold in 2025 feeding the same review pipeline; 70+ plaintiffs joined the litigation by September 2026.

**Company Role:** Direct — Meta designed the data pipeline, contracted the reviewers, and wrote the disputed privacy marketing claims.

**Case Status:** Active / Ongoing.

Meta faces a class action (filed March 2026 in San Francisco federal court) alleging its marketing for Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses ("designed for privacy, controlled by you") misrepresented how footage is handled. Reporting by a Swedish newspaper found Kenya-based contractors reviewing intimate footage — including bathroom visits and sexual activity — as part of Meta's AI-training pipeline, without adequately disclosing this to users. Meta says footage stays on-device unless users choose to share it; plaintiffs dispute the consistency of Meta's stated safeguards. The UK Information Commissioner's Office has also opened a separate investigation.

## 2. AI-Driven Layoff Discrimination Lawsuit — Orange Flag (Score: 1, Severity: Severe)

**Category / Pillar:** Social — Labor Rights & Supply Chain : Discrimination & Workforce Diversity

**Nature of Harm:** Serious — job loss, lost equity, and lost income tied to an AI-scoring system that structurally penalized employees on protected leave, including a scientist terminated two days before giving birth.

**Scale of Impact:** Extensive — 26 named plaintiffs, drawn from an ~8,000-role (10% of workforce) reduction-in-force where the same scoring methodology was applied broadly.

**Company Role:** Direct — Meta built and deployed the internal AI/monitoring systems used to select employees for termination.

**Case Status:** Active / Ongoing.

26 current and former Meta employees sued in July 2026, alleging that internal AI systems ("Metamate," keystroke/activity monitoring, AI-token dashboards, algorithmic performance ranking) disproportionately selected staff on FMLA, pregnancy-disability, or other protected leave, and employees with disability accommodations, for a May 2026 mass layoff. A federal judge declined to block the layoffs but found the case raised "serious questions going to the merits." Meta maintains that "workforce management and organizational decisions were and are made by people, not AI."

## 3. Scam Ads / "Epidemic of Fraud" — Orange Flag (Score: 1, Severity: Severe)

**Category / Pillar:** Governance — Bribery & Fraud

**Nature of Harm:** Serious — an estimated $16B in 2024 revenue tied to scam advertising and a Reuters-documented internal "playbook" allegedly used to mislead regulators about the true scale of the problem.

**Scale of Impact:** Extremely Widespread — roughly a third of all successful scams in the US, ~15 billion scam ads shown daily worldwide, and regulatory/legal action spanning the US, UK, and Europe.

**Company Role:** Direct — the alleged Ad Library manipulation and limited advertiser-verification rollout were Meta's own internal policy choices.

**Case Status:** Active / Ongoing.

A Reuters investigation found Meta allegedly built an internal "playbook" to game its own Ad Library so that ads flagged by regulator keyword searches were removed from those searches specifically, without meaningfully reducing the overall volume of scam ads — a strategy a former Meta fraud investigator called "regulatory theatre." Separately, three users sued over crypto-scam ads that routed them to fraudulent WhatsApp groups, and the UK's Chartered Trading Standards Institute publicly called on government to act on Meta's "epidemic of fraud," citing £39M in UK consumer losses in 2024 alone. Meta's own internal analysis reportedly found universal advertiser verification would cost ~$2B and cut revenue up to 4.8%, and it has only implemented verification where legally mandated.

## 4. Child Safety & Social Media Addiction Settlement — Yellow Flag (Score: 2, Severity: Severe)

**Category / Pillar:** Social — Customers : Product Safety & Quality

**Nature of Harm:** Serious — platforms allegedly designed to be addictive to minors, causing documented mental-health harm.

**Scale of Impact:** Extremely Widespread — 47 states, thousands of families, and over 100,000 pending lawsuits.

**Company Role:** Direct — the alleged addictive design (infinite scroll, engagement optimization, data collection on minors) is Meta's own product choice.

**Case Status:** Active / Partially Concluded.

Meta agreed to pay up to $17.1 billion and change how teens use its platforms, settling a landmark case led by a bipartisan coalition of 29+ state attorneys general who accused Facebook and Instagram of being designed to keep children hooked while collecting their data. The settlement follows earlier losses, including a $6 million bellwether jury verdict and a separate $567 million New Mexico court order. Critics note the settlement is roughly 2% of the trillions in damages some models estimated, admits no wrongdoing, is contingent on YouTube and TikTok also settling, and features only "best-effort" age verification. Governance critics also point to Mark Zuckerberg's ~61% voting control (via a dual-class share structure) as what allowed years of shareholder warnings on this issue to be overridden.

## 5. Muse Image AI Consent Backlash — Green Flag (Score: 6, Severity: Moderate)

**Category / Pillar:** Social — Customers : Privacy & Data Security

**Nature of Harm:** Medium — default opt-in use of public Instagram accounts' likenesses for AI image generation, without affirmative consent, though no confirmed exploitation was reported before withdrawal.

**Scale of Impact:** Extensive — enabled by default for all public Instagram accounts, though live for only about three days.

**Company Role:** Direct.

**Case Status:** Active / Concluded.

Meta launched "Muse Image" on July 7, 2026, letting users @-mention any public Instagram account to have Meta AI reference that account's photos when generating new images — enabled by default, requiring users to opt out. Public Citizen called it an "egregious invasion" of privacy; CAA and SAG-AFTRA pushed back, with the union urging members to opt out. Meta disabled the feature entirely on July 10, 2026, citing user confusion, with no announced plans to reintroduce it.

**Company Role:** Direct.

**Case Status:** Active / Ongoing.

## 8. Instagram End-to-End Encryption Removal — Green Flag (Score: 8, Severity: Minor)

**Category / Pillar:** Social — Customers : Privacy & Data Security

**Nature of Harm:** Medium — removing E2E encryption increases theoretical exposure of private message content, though no concrete resulting harm was reported.

**Scale of Impact:** Low — Meta stated "very few people" had opted into the encrypted-DM feature being removed.

**Company Role:** Direct.

**Case Status:** Active / Concluded.

Meta discontinued end-to-end encryption for Instagram private messages effective May 8, 2026, reversing a 2019 commitment. Meta cited low opt-in rates and pointed users toward WhatsApp for encrypted messaging; child-safety and law-enforcement groups (including the Virtual Global Taskforce) had pushed for the change, arguing encryption hampered detection of child sexual abuse material, while privacy advocates criticized the reversal as reducing message confidentiality.
