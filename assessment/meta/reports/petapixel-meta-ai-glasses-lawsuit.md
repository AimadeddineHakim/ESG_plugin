Meta faces a class action lawsuit filed March 4, 2026 in federal court in San Francisco regarding its Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses. The lawsuit alleges the company misled consumers about privacy protections for footage captured by the devices.

The legal challenge follows a Swedish newspaper report revealing that contractors in Kenya review footage used to train Meta's AI systems. These workers reported encountering intimate content including bathroom visits and sexual encounters.

The lawsuit argues Meta failed to disclose that human reviewers analyze captured footage. The complaint states the company engaged in "affirmatively false advertising and failure to disclose the true nature of surveillance."

The case was filed by Clarkson Law Firm on behalf of two plaintiffs: Gina Bartone of New Jersey and Mateo Canu of California, who purchased the glasses based on privacy marketing claims.

The lawsuit contends that "Meta employees and contractors have described viewing credit card numbers, nudity, sexual activity, and identifiable faces in the footage."

Meta acknowledged using contractors but maintained that "unless users choose to share media they've captured with Meta or others, that media stays on the user's device." However, workers involved in the review process disputed the effectiveness of Meta's filtering systems in preventing private content exposure. By September 2026, more than 70 people who purchased, used, or were allegedly recorded through Meta smart glasses had joined suits against the company.
