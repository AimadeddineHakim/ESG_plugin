Three Meta Platforms users filed a class-action lawsuit in San Francisco federal court alleging they were defrauded through Facebook and Instagram advertisements promoting cryptocurrency schemes.

The plaintiffs — Niroshini Dassanayake (Florida), Kenneth Gugel (Georgia), and Andrew Svoboda (Illinois) — claim the platform's ads directed them to WhatsApp groups operated by scammers. These bad actors then persuaded them to transfer money or cryptocurrency to digital wallets. When attempting withdrawals, the plaintiffs discovered that "the displayed balances were illusory and the deposited funds were gone."

The complaint contends Meta's generative AI capabilities enhanced fraudulent advertising effectiveness by creating hundreds of optimized variations targeting vulnerable consumers. Plaintiffs assert Meta knew or should have known its AI tools "contribute to the creation and optimization of false and deceptive advertisements."

Prior legal context: Meta faced a similar 2025 lawsuit involving WhatsApp pump-and-dump schemes. Though a judge initially rejected Meta's Section 230 immunity defense — finding the company played too significant a role in ad creation — that earlier case was ultimately dismissed based on federal securities law preemption.
