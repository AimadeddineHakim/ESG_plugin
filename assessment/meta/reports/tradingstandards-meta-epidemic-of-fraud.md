Meta generated an estimated $16 billion in revenue from scam advertisements globally in 2024, representing approximately 10% of its total revenue. UK consumers lost £28 million to Facebook scam ads and £11 million to Instagram scam ads in 2024. Meta's platforms display an estimated 15 billion scam advertisements daily worldwide.

The UK Financial Conduct Authority discovered over 1,000 unauthorized high-risk financial product advertisements on Meta's platforms during a single week in November 2025. A Santa Clara County lawsuit alleges Meta knowingly hosted fraudulent content and facilitated an "epidemic of fraud."

The Chartered Trading Standards Institute (CTSI) argues that Meta knowingly hosts fraudulent advertisements while profiting from them. Despite commitments to prevent unauthorized financial product promotions, Meta has repeatedly failed compliance — 56% of flagged ads came from previously identified offenders.

CTSI demands: substantial financial penalties against social media platforms enabling fraud; increased resource allocation for removing fraudulent content; immediate removal of content flagged by Trading Standards and regulators; and enhanced government accountability measures.

Key quotes:
- John Herriman, CTSI Chief Executive: "Meta and its platforms sit at the top of a pyramid of fraudulent adverts...The profits...is eye-watering."
- Katherine Hart, Lead Officer: "Consumers are misled by adverts for shops or items that don't exist...Meta has a duty."
- Louise Baxter MBE, Lead Officer: "Fraud has become a volume crime at an industrial scale...we must shift upstream."
