Meta faces a class action lawsuit in the United States over privacy violations related to its AI-enabled smart glasses. The legal action follows investigations revealing that overseas subcontractors reviewed sensitive footage from users' devices.

A Swedish newspaper investigation discovered that employees at a Kenya-based subcontractor examined footage captured by Meta's smart glasses, including intimate content such as nudity and people using bathrooms. Meta had claimed to blur faces in images, but sources disputed the consistency of this protection.

The lawsuit was filed by plaintiffs Gina Bartone of New Jersey and Mateo Canu of California, represented by the Clarkson Law Firm. The complaint alleges Meta violated consumer protection laws and engaged in false advertising.

Meta advertised the glasses with slogans including "designed for privacy, controlled by you" and "built for your privacy." The lawsuit contends these marketing messages misled consumers who believed their footage would remain private and not be reviewed by workers overseas. The complaint notes: "You're in control of your data and content," emphasizing that users believed they controlled which content was shared.

Meta's response: the company stated that contractors review shared content to improve user experience, a practice explained in their privacy policy. A company spokesperson said the glasses help users "use AI, hands-free" and that "unless users choose to share media...that media stays on the user's device."

Scale and context: over seven million people purchased Meta smart glasses in 2025, with their footage fed into a data pipeline for review; users cannot opt out of this process. The U.K. Information Commissioner's Office has also launched an investigation into the matter.
