Meta has agreed to pay up to $17.1 billion to settle claims from 47 states and thousands of families asserting that Facebook and Instagram were designed to addict children.

The author, Andrew Behar (CEO of As You Sow), documents shareholder activism efforts stretching back years:
- 2019: Filed a resolution documenting 45+ million child sexual abuse images found on Facebook.
- 2020: Faith-based investors presented a sex-trafficking survivor at Meta's annual meeting and filed a "Reboot Facebook" proposal.
- 2021: A content governance resolution won 63.1% of independent shareholder votes but only 19% overall, because of Mark Zuckerberg's outsized voting control.

Core governance problem: Zuckerberg controls approximately 61% of voting power while owning just 13% of the company, through a dual-class stock structure where he receives 10 votes for every share held by an ordinary shareholder.

Critical issues with the settlement:
- It represents only roughly 2% of what plaintiff damages models estimated could reach trillions of dollars.
- The settlement is contingent on YouTube and TikTok also participating in similar arrangements.
- Age-verification requirements are "best-effort" only, allowing workarounds.
- Meta admits no wrongdoing while the settlement structure entrenches its market dominance.

The author argues the underlying governance failure — concentrated, unaccountable voting control — is what allowed the harms to persist for years despite repeated shareholder warnings, and advocates eliminating dual-class share structures in favor of "one share, one vote."
