According to a Reuters investigation, Meta Platforms Inc. developed an internal "playbook" to evade regulatory pressure regarding scam advertisements on Facebook and Instagram. The reporting drew on internal documents from Meta's finance, legal, public policy, and safety teams spanning four years.

Main tactics: Meta allegedly manipulated its Ad Library — a searchable database meant to promote transparency — by identifying keywords regulators used and repeatedly running searches to remove flagged fraudulent ads from those specific results. This approach reduced the appearance of problematic ads in regulator searches without significantly decreasing the overall volume of scam advertisements on the platform.

Cybersecurity consultant Sandeep Abraham, formerly a fraud investigator at Meta, characterized this strategy as "regulatory theatre" that undermined the Ad Library's purpose. Meta disputed this characterization, stating removals were tied to genuine enforcement efforts.

Financial motivation: Meta's own internal analyses indicated that implementing universal advertiser verification would cost approximately $2 billion and reduce revenue by up to 4.8%. Instead of accepting these losses, the company adopted a reactive approach, implementing verification only where legally required, such as in Singapore and Taiwan.

Broader impact: Meta only introduced advertiser verification in markets with legal mandates. High-risk ads reportedly generate the company up to $7 billion annually, which the investigation cites as a likely explanation for its hesitancy toward stricter verification rules. The U.S. Virgin Islands subsequently sued Meta over ad scams, and European regulators requested additional compliance details from the company.
