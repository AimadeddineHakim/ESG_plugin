A landmark trial began with opening statements in federal court, brought by 29 state attorneys general. The lawsuit accuses Meta of designing Facebook and Instagram to encourage infinite scrolling and addiction among young users while collecting their data.

Potential financial exposure: states sought $200 billion in damages; Meta itself said potential exposure could reach $1.4 trillion given the scale of claims. Meta's 2025 revenue was approximately $201 billion with $83.2 billion in operating income. Prior related penalties already levied against Meta include a $375 million New Mexico lawsuit judgment (March 2026) and a separate $567 million order from a judge.

The trial was expected to last six weeks. Plaintiffs wanted Meta to eliminate infinite scroll functionality, remove algorithms built on minor data, and implement time restrictions for young users, arguing Meta's existing protective features are insufficient because teens can easily dismiss notifications.

Meta stated: "Our financial performance has been significantly determined by our success in adding, retaining, and engaging active users" that deliver ad impressions.

Tre Lovell, a media law attorney, predicted: "We're going to get close to some type of global settlement, a global resolution." Boston College's Aleksandar Tomic compared the case to tobacco litigation: "This is the tobacco litigation of the information age."

Meta faces over 100,000 lawsuits from individuals, cities, states, and school districts; similar cases target Snap, TikTok, and YouTube.
