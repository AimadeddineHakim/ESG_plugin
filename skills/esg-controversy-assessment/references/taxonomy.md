# ESG Controversy Taxonomy

Reference definitions for detecting and classifying an ESG controversy. Use these definitions verbatim when assessing text.

## 1. Controversy detection

An ESG controversy case is defined as either an event or an ongoing situation in which company operations and/or products allegedly have a negative environmental, social and/or governance impact.

Cases include alleged company violations of existing laws and/or regulations to which they are subject, or an alleged company action or event that violates commonly accepted international norms, including but not limited to global norms and conventions such as the International Labour Organization (ILO) Fundamental Conventions.

A case can be a single event (a spill, accident, or regulatory action) or a set of closely linked events/allegations (e.g. health and safety fines at the same facility, multiple allegations of anticompetitive behavior related to the same product line, multiple community protests at the same company location, or multiple individual lawsuits alleging the same type of discrimination).

Answer: **Positive** (a controversy is detected) or **Negative** (no controversy detected).

## 2. Primary ESG category

If a controversy is detected, classify its primary category:

1. **Environmental** — the company's impact on nature, climate, or the use/management of natural resources (habitat destruction, pollution, carbon emissions, resource depletion).
2. **Social** — the company's interactions with customers, communities, or its workforce (labor rights, customer safety, data privacy, community well-being).
3. **Governance** — the company's ethical practices, corporate governance structures, or financial integrity (corruption, fraud, conflicts of interest, board oversight issues).

## 3. Environmental subcategories

- **Biodiversity & Land Use** — species loss, habitat damage, depletion of natural resources, or post-consumer waste, especially in sensitive areas. Excludes toxic emissions and water competition.
- **Toxic Emissions & Waste** — toxic or hazardous emissions/releases to land, air, or water, including spills and operational emissions.
- **Energy & Climate Change** — greenhouse gas emissions, climate-related lawsuits, or refusal to address climate impact.
- **Water Stress** — water depletion, competition for water resources, or disputes over water use.
- **Operational Waste (Non-Hazardous)** — non-toxic operational waste not classified under emissions or biodiversity.
- **Supply Chain Management** — raw material sourcing leading to significant environmental degradation (e.g. palm oil, tropical hardwood).
- **Other** — environmentally focused impact that fits none of the above.

## 4. Governance subcategories

- **Bribery & Fraud** — ethics violations including bribery, tax evasion, insider trading.
- **Governance Structures** — issues with board management, conflicts of interest, or shareholder misalignment.
- **Controversial Investments** — lending, underwriting, or financing with negative social/environmental impact.
- **Other** — governance issues not captured above.

## 5. Social pillars and sub-pillars

**Customers**
- Anticompetitive Practices — price fixing, bid rigging, predatory pricing.
- Marketing & Advertising — deceptive marketing, off-label use promotion, marketing to vulnerable groups.
- Product Safety & Quality — safety/quality concerns, recalls, controversial content.
- Customer Relations — improper billing, hidden fees, discriminatory access.
- Privacy & Data Security — data privacy breaches or controversial data policies.
- Other — customer-related social issues not covered above.

**Human Rights & Community Impact**
- Impact on Communities — land disputes, access to resources, economic impacts from operations.
- Civil Liberties — restrictions on freedoms due to company activities (censorship, surveillance).
- Human Rights Concerns — violations or complicity in severe rights violations.
- Other — community/human-rights issues not covered above.

**Labor Rights & Supply Chain**
- Labor Management Relations — workplace disputes, mistreatment, forced labor.
- Health & Safety — workplace injuries or other physical risks to employees.
- Collective Bargaining & Unions — anti-union activities or contract disputes.
- Discrimination & Workforce Diversity — discrimination based on demographic characteristics.
- Child Labor — allegations of child labor within the company or supply chain.
- Supply Chain Labor Standards — labor issues in the supply chain (unsafe conditions, forced labor, discrimination).
- Other — labor-related issues not covered above.

Answer format for Social: `"Pillar" : "Sub-Pillar"` (e.g. `Labor Rights & Supply Chain : Health & Safety`).
